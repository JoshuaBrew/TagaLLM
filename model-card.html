Model details

The evaluated local app used original Google Gemma 4 E2B in Q4 with TagaLLM’s conversation prompt, cultural references, and language and scope checks. It is designed for everyday conversation. Long exchanges can lose track of updated details, and the language check can occasionally misclassify short replies.

The saved adapter training assets pin Google Gemma 4 E2B IT revision 4adf15748de9e1a0097b6274cee9c15836c41c37. The downloadable Q4 file matches the evaluated checkpoint checksum. Google lists Apache 2.0 for upstream Gemma 4. Google’s model card

Fine-tuning experiments

We trained experimental LoRA adapters on selected reviewed Tagalog dialogues. In the 22-dialogue TV pilot, held-out assistant loss fell from 4.44 to 3.72. Base Gemma and the adapted model each fully answered 2 of 6 final requests. The app configuration with cultural references answered 4 of 6 on that same development suite. The evaluated app and downloadable setup use base weights. The adapter is a separate research artifact.

Evaluation

During TagaLLM development, a native Tagalog speaker helped review selected model replies for grammar and natural phrasing, with AI assistance.

Our development harness sends the next user message after each generated reply and records raw text, latency, relevance, naturalness, and state tracking. The saved E2B reference-enabled run passed all six exchanges in 15% of chats under editorial scoring. See the article for denominators and examples.

A six-exchange pass requires all six replies to earn 2 out of 2 for naturalness and relevance, plus 2 out of 2 on every applicable state-tracking check. Reviewers record a note when state tracking does not apply. The harness requires a nonempty reply on every turn.

Across the 120 logged replies in the reference-enabled E2B development run, the local app took a median of 2.16 seconds per reply. The 95th percentile was 3.03 seconds. These timings come from the guarded development endpoint on the test machine.

Recorded license and attribution details

The frozen prose metadata labels 14 journalism excerpts CC BY 3.0 and one Wikipedia excerpt CC BY-SA 4.0. The 35 storybook entries carry CC BY 3.0 in their separate license URL field, with credits and source links for all 13 titles. All 13 linked publisher pages display CC BY 3.0 and match the archived translator credits.

Voice of America Learning English published all four transcripts in the Conversation sections of its Let’s Learn English – Level 1 lessons. The dates below are those displayed on the lesson pages. VOA credits certified American English teachers with designing the course, but the reviewed lesson pages did not identify individual transcript writers, credit a third party for the dialogue text, or provide a transcript-specific copyright notice. VOA’s terms identify exclusively VOA-produced material as public domain and ask users to give credit. They reserve rights for third-party material. If a transcript is confirmed to fall under the public-domain policy, U.S. copyright would not restrict translation, adaptation, copying for training, or redistribution of that transcript. The reviewed sources do not establish that condition or provide separate transcript-specific permission for these uses. The intake records’ approval flags document project decisions and do not establish rights clearance. VOA terms of use · U.S. Copyright Office, 17 U.S.C. §§ 101, 105–107

Private research and training require their own assessment. Research can qualify as fair use under U.S. law depending on the circumstances. Reporting aggregate findings and linking to the lessons can proceed independently of distributing adapted dialogue. Hold these four rows out of any public dataset export until exclusive production is confirmed or applicable permission is documented. The audit does not establish worldwide reuse rights.
